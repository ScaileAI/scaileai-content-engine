Did you know the callback is where the profit on a job usually goes?

You priced the work, you did it, and it was fine. Then two weeks later something is not right, or they did not understand what you told them, or a part failed. You go back. You do not charge for it, because you cannot really.

One of those turns a good job into an average one. Two turns it into a loss, and nobody writes it down anywhere, so it never shows up in the numbers.

Most callbacks are not workmanship. They are expectation. The customer did not know what normal looked like afterwards, so the first odd noise became a fault.

A short message a few days later explaining what to expect prevents a surprising number of them. It costs nothing and it has to be sent on a day you have already moved on.

Save this one for the next time you are driving back to a job you already finished.
